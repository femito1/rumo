`test_convenio_memo_is_ignored_when_it_does_not_describe_this_month`) so a memo that cites

**What I am unsure about is what to use INSTEAD.** Three options, measured:

posted plan is 2.122,30 in all six months, so the "Parte MBC" that goes with that plan

I think a next agent should not switch to C on their own — it would tie the book, which is

**Also note B made the reported per-área differences LOOK worse** while making each line

## 3. What I think about 35,52 — and why I stopped

I think **it does not exist in SISJURI**, and I think that is now about as well-established
as it can be from our side:

- full untruncated histórico (after the v4 widening + re-extract) reads literally
  `"Vale refeição"` / `"Vale transporte"` for January — no calculation tail;
- the string appears in no field of any of the eight 2026 months, nor the twelve 2025 ones;
- not in the May `.xls` extrato, the June extrato PDF, the Jan–Mai PPTX, the AR
  demonstrativo, or any Excel cell/threaded comment (the workbook has 14 comments and they
  *do* annotate neighbouring rows — "IBRAC", "aasp", "Aluguel - valor pago Belline" — but
  none on r122/r123);
- not a whole number of days at any known rate, nor a sum of two.

**A hypothesis I could not kill:** `35,52 = 4 × 8,88` exactly, and January is the month
where VR was paid for 18 days while MLA's VT covered only 14 — a four-day gap. I *think*
this is coincidence, because 8,88 is nobody's daily rate and the day-count gap is +3..+5 in
every month (so January's +4 is normal, not anomalous). But I would not bet heavily on it.

**A hypothesis I killed, and I think the kill is solid:** that 35,52 is a typo for 37,52
(= 2 × 18,76, which would make January exactly 16 VT days). At 16 days the VR−VT gap
becomes +2, which occurs in no other month. So MLA really had 14 commuting days and our
262,64 is complete. Test: `test_every_vale_row_is_a_whole_number_of_days`.

I stopped because I ran out of sources, not because I am certain. While writing this I
noticed I had only searched 2025 and 2026, so I checked **2024 as well** (all twelve months
are in the store with real data): `35,52`, `8,88` and `17,76` are absent there too. So the
store holds 32 months of data and the number is in none of them.

The one source I do **not** have is a **January** raw export — the `.xls` and PDFs we hold
are May and June only. If anyone wants a final answer without asking Renata, that is the
thing to fetch. I would rate the chance it helps as low, since the January snapshot's
histórico is now full-length and bare.

*(Small aside from the 2024 sweep, possibly useful someday: the 2024 vale históricos use a
different wording — `"Vale refeição MLA - Ref a outubro 23 dias úteis"` — and reference the
FOLLOWING month's competence. If anyone ever needs to parse vale text across years, do not
assume the 2026 `"Calculo: N dias x R$ X"` shape holds.)*

## 4. Things I believe but have NOT pinned with a test

Flagging these deliberately, because they are load-bearing for the client document:

- **I think the r204/205/206 formula shift is the dominant Despesas-Equipe cause.** Measured:
  recomputing Jan–Mai with June's formula cuts absolute error 66% and takes ties from 4/18
  to 11/18 (`scripts/audit_despesas_area.py`). That is a strong number but it is a script
  output, not an assertion — nothing fails if it regresses.
- **I think the per-área Despesa Institucional difference is entirely the POOL, not the
  áreas.** The share effect sums to zero in every month and the split is exact to R$0,01
  (`scripts/audit_desp_inst_rateio.py`). Same caveat: script, not test. ⚠ I had this
  attributed to the formula shift in an earlier version of the document and it was **wrong**
  — if you touch this, re-read that script's docstring first.
- **I think January's remaining despesas-área residual is fully the Associações gap**
  (AASP 195,40 + Canal de Arbitragem 1.204,47 = 1.399,87, matching to the centavo). Derived
  by hand from the workbook + DB, not asserted anywhere.
- **I think Jan/Mar/Abr are unprotected.** Fixtures exist only for Fev/Mai/Jun, so the
  numbers in the client document for the other months could drift with no test failing.
  The user deferred this ("dont worry about fixtures for now") and I think that was
  reasonable at the time, but it is the biggest structural gap I am leaving behind.

## 5. Mistakes I made this session, so they are not repeated

I am listing these because each cost real time and each is a pattern, not a one-off:

1. **I declared 543,22 "not found anywhere" when it was in the data** — 507,10 + 36,12 on
   `020.080.0050/0060`, and already written down in `docs/SISJURI_DB.md`. My sweep tested
   values and pairwise *differences* but never *sums*. **Search for compositions, and grep
   the durable docs before declaring anything unknown.**
2. **I "fixed" the K-rounding by adding a decimal, and made a real row worse.** Rounded
   parts never sum to a rounded total at any precision (~49% of 6-month rows diverge at one
   decimal *and* at two). Measure before assuming precision helps; the deck now discloses
   the rounding instead.
3. **I mis-attributed per-área Despesa Institucional to the formula shift.** It is the pool.
   Decompose before naming a cause.
4. **I trusted a subagent's report of a R$150k Impostos Orçado discrepancy.** The live
   budget has `imposto = 100.750,00` = exactly 15% × recebimento. The claim came from a
   fixture. **Check live before planning work around a report.**
5. **I put the workbook's figure in an operator runbook where ours belonged** (Contencioso
   Despesa Institucional 32.562,84 vs our 32.564,56). An operator would have chased the
   R$1,72 bank tariff as a regression.
6. **I nearly had the operator run a re-extract while the new SQL was unpushed.** The agent
   self-updates `extract.sql` from GitHub `main`, and `backfill.ps1` never passes
   `-NoSelfUpdate`, so every month would have silently re-downloaded v3.
